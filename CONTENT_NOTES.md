# Content notes

Review notes on the 72 drafted options in `content/pools.json` (12 per category). Delete this file once the content is settled; the site doesn't use it.

## Options I think are weak

**Subgenre: Epic Quest.** The most generic option and the one students will default to in open mode. It's useful as a safe card, but it gives the least to push against. Candidate for replacement (e.g. *Survival*, *Heist*, *Picaresque*).

**Subgenre: Coming of Age.** More a theme than a subgenre, and it overlaps with the *Apprentice* role. I widened the description so it works for an old character too ("crosses a line they can never uncross"), but it remains the softest subgenre.

**Subgenre: Sword and Sorcery.** The name promises sorcery, which collides with *No Magic* / *Magic Is a Con*. The description avoids assuming magic, and the collision is a workable challenge, but non-native speakers may read the title literally and feel stuck.

**Magic: Inherited Gift.** A familiar trope (magic aristocracy). It works, but it's the least surprising card in the pool.

**Technology: Early Iron.** Hard for students to distinguish from *Age of Bronze* and *Castles and Mills*. It's there to keep the range continuous; cut it first if you want a more distinctive pool.

**Technology: Uneven Progress.** The most abstract card: it asks the student to invent the mismatch themselves. Strong students will love it; weaker ones may stall. Consider replacing the examples with one fixed, concrete mismatch.

**Setting: The Mountain Stair.** The thinnest setting: it describes a place but suggests less daily life and conflict than the others.

**Role: Apprentice.** Generic, and close to *Coming of Age*. Fine as a gentle card, but the least specific role.

**Conflict: A Loving Lie / The Witness.** Both hinge on a hidden truth. They feel different on the page, but if you want maximum spread, replace one (e.g. with *A Debt Comes Due* or *The Rival*).

## Combinability checks worth knowing about

These combinations are deliberately possible and may need a word in class:

- **Mythic Epic + No Magic.** "Gods walk among mortals" can't be literal. Read it as mortals mythologised into gods, or gods as a belief that shapes behaviour. A good discussion point.
- **City of a Million + Stone and Bone.** A huge city without metal. Intentionally strange.
- **Living Ink + Stone and Bone.** The description says "written or painted in rare pigments" so cave painting counts.
- **Court Intrigue + Open Steppe / Stone and Bone.** "Court" can be a khan's camp or a chief's fire.
- **Hunger Season** says "harvest or hunt" so it works for non-farming societies.
- **Something Is Coming:** I avoided "migrating people" as the threat, to steer away from framing migrants as invaders.

## Vocabulary that may need pre-teaching

For Dutch-speaking 17–18-year-olds, these are the words most likely to slow students down: *sleight of hand*, *automatons*, *pigments*, *hearths*, *besieged*, *despised*, *comrades*, *aqueducts*, *quarrels*, *informers*. None are archaisms, but a quick glossary on the board would help.

## Spelling

British spelling throughout (*colour*, *civilisation*, *ploughs*, *travellers*, *organised*). Keep it consistent if you add options.
